A famous name earns no extra points. An alternative can be worth your attention through a close match, a better community rating, or a worthwhile tradeoff.

What the ratings tell you

These are Fragrantica community ratings, shown to three decimals with their vote counts. They measure general liking. They do not establish female preference or an overall best-fragrance ranking. Sorting by rating uses the full recorded value before display rounding.

What makes an alternative worth exploring

The screen looks for well-supported similarity, a higher paired community rating, or a substantial reported price difference. Disputed mappings and thin evidence are excluded. Similarity agreement describes votes, not a percentage of identical ingredients. The candidates still need item-level review.

How the selection was collected

The first 500 positions come from one combined male and unisex Fragrantica Most popular search, acquired through API.SH. There are no category quotas. The first ten match the supplied screenshot; the full native 500-position order has not been independently verified. Popularity defines the research pool, not quality.

Female preference and the proposed model

The proposed mainstream designer policy gives female preference 65%, male preference 20%, and mixed or unknown-audience evidence 15%. The specialist policy uses 40%, 40%, and 20%. These weights are not applied to the community ratings shown here. Missing female evidence is not zero. A perfume's male or unisex category never identifies its voters.

Your shortlist stays personal

The comparison tray is stored in this browser. Your comparisons, purchases, sampling and personal preferences never change the public ratings or the ranking model.
